Not legal advice: licence readings are mine from the texts quoted; the lead / user decides the risk.

## 1. Summary

1. **Shadertoy's default is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, i.e. not usable for a paid show** unless the shader's own header says otherwise (MIT, CC0, etc.) or the author gives written permission. The terms say the author owns the shader, "decides which license applies", and "if you don't place a license on a shader, it will be protected by our default license" CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 ([terms, archive copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://www.shadertoy.com/terms)). NC = "not primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or private monetary compensation" ([CC legalcode 4(c)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/legalcode)); a fee-earning festival job is the textbook case CC's own guidance points at ([CC NC interpretation](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/NonCommercial_interpretation): context and purpose decide; gray areas exist). SA also forces any adaptation to be shared under the same licence (legalcode 4(b)).
2. **Safe, verified-permissive core** (MIT unless noted): Gustavson/McEwan noise (`snoise`, `psrdnoise`), Inigo Quilez's article snippets (SDFs, smooth-min, palettes, domain warp), three.js shader chunks (thin-film iridescence, 3-sample RGB dispersion), Vidvox ISF-Files (repo MIT), gl-transitions (per-file MIT), and the MIT-headed subset of lygia.
3. **Not usable as-is:** The Book of Shaders (all rights reserved; "cannot use this Work in any commercial or non-commercial product"), hg_sdf (CC BY-NC), most of lygia (Prosperity 3.0.0: 30-day commercial trial only), GLSL Sandbox shaders (no licence, so copyright default), Shadertoy default-licence shaders, any repo with no LICENSE.
4. **lygia is a trap for a paid show:** the repo licence is Prosperity Public License 3.0.0 (non-commercial; "thirty-day" commercial trial) dual with a Patron licence for sponsors/contributors. Per file: 375 of 652 `.glsl` carry a Prosperity header, 77 carry another licence (mostly MIT, a few BSD/CC BY), 200 carry none (I assume they inherit the repo licence; **unverified**). Use only the MIT-headed files or pay/contribute (the user's call).
5. IQ's own rule: "all technical code snippets you'll find are under the MIT license so you can easily reuse them, but the mathematical/shader art is protected and requires a license for use" ([iquilezles.org/articles](https://iquilezles.org/articles/)). So SDF primitives, smin, palette, warp functions: fine. His finished Shadertoy artworks: not.
6. **25 picks in section 4** are all permissive on the evidence I could read; each lists the exact licence line and where it was seen.
7. TD port basics (all already verified in this repo): no `#version`, `vUV.st`, `uTDOutputInfo.res` (z/w = width/height), `sTD2DInputs[]`, output through `TDOutputSwizzle`, max 3 wired inputs, rename any variable called `active`. See section 6.
8. Practical rule proposed: every ported shader gets a header comment (source URL, author, licence text, date read) and a row in a licence ledger; anything with an unverifiable licence is rewritten from the maths or skipped.

## 3. Per-source detail

### 3.1 Shadertoy - what the default licence means for a paid show
- Source of the rule: terms text "What license will my Shaders have? All the shaders you create in Shadertoy are owned by you. You decide which license applies to every shader you create ... if you don't place a license on a shader, it will be protected by our default license: ... Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported License." Also: "users retain all rights to their creations and can decide a specific license for it" ([archive copy](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://www.shadertoy.com/terms)). I could not open the live page (403).
- **BY:** keep the author's name/notices (CC 3.0 legalcode 4(d)).
- **NC:** "You may not exercise any of the rights granted to You ... in any manner that is primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or private monetary compensation" ([legalcode](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/legalcode)). CC adds that it looks at purpose and context, not at who you are, and that there are gray areas; its example of a restricted use is an NC song inside a commercially distributed music video ([CC wiki](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/NonCommercial_interpretation)). A shader ported into TD and rendered into show visuals for a fee is closer to that example than to a free hobby clip. Whether the festival counts as commercial for *this* use is a legal call; I did not find a source that settles "paid VJ show" either way. A Vidvox thread shows community norms: a port keeps the original licence and re-licensing needs the author's consent ([thread](https://discourse.vidvox.net/t/user-bennoh-is-stealing-shaders-and-uploading-them-online-with-mit-license/2682)).
- **SA:** an Adaptation may only be distributed or publicly performed under the same licence (legalcode 4(b)). A TD port is an Adaptation of the code. What that means for a rendered video clip is **not answered by anything I read - UNVERIFIED**, a question for counsel, not for us.
- Ways out, in order of cost: (1) use shaders whose own header is MIT/CC0/public domain; (2) email/comment the author and keep the written "yes, commercial OK" with the ledger; (3) rewrite the effect yourself from the maths (technique is not the code; **general principle, not sourced here**); (4) skip it.
- Shadertoy IDs that sit inside MIT-licensed text elsewhere (use only after opening the page by hand to confirm the header): IQ noise family `4dXBRH, XdXBRH, XsXfRH, 4dffRH, lsf3WH, 4sfGzS, XdXGW8, Xsl3Dl, Msf3WH` (listed in the MIT-headed value-noise code inside Vidvox's Dirty Lens ISF), Martijn Steinrucken `wlSBzD` (spectral) and `wsBfzK` (wavelet) (lygia headers say MIT 2020), Hoskins hash `4djSRW` (lygia says MIT, 2014), IQ cosine palette `Xl2GRc` (linked from the MIT article).

### 3.2 lygia - per-file reality
- Repo licence and dual-licence text: see table. Prosperity 3.0.0 core sentences: "free ... for noncommercial purposes", "try this software for commercial purposes for thirty days", "Limit your use of this software for commercial purposes to a thirty-day trial period" ([LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/patriciogonzalezvivo/lygia/main/LICENSE.md)).
- Patron route: README says "Making a PR or subscribing to the GitHub sponsors program is the shortest path to get access to the commercial license"; terms page lygia.xyz/license returned HTTP 500 when I fetched it, so **the Patron text itself is unseen**; contact in README is the author's email. Spending/sponsoring is the user's decision.
- My count over all 652 `.glsl` files (2026-10-16): 375 Prosperity header; 77 other (nearly all MIT: Gustavson/McEwan noise `snoise cnoise pnoise psrdnoise`, Hoskins `random`, DesLauriers `grain`, Jamie Owen blend modes, Ottosson Oklab, Martijn Steinrucken `spectral` and `wavelet`, Shadi El Hajj lighting/space utilities, IQ `triPrismSDF`; plus a BSD-2 `smartDeNoise` and CC BY 3.0 `hammersley`); 200 with no licence field - e.g. `sdf/sphereSDF.glsl` says only "contributors: Inigo Quiles" (IQ's code, MIT on his site, but lygia gives no licence line), `generative/curl.glsl`, `generative/voronoise.glsl`, `generative/noised.glsl`. **Do not copy a "none" file without tracing it to the original source.**
- `color/palette/spectral/` helper files: `zucconi*.glsl` and `gems.glsl` have no licence line (Alan Zucconi blog / NVIDIA GPU Gems origin) - avoid; the main `spectral(float)` in `spectral.glsl` is MIT (Martijn Steinrucken).
- Note `lygia/sdf/*` and `generative/voronoi,worley,fbm,gnoise` are Prosperity or unlicensed. Take the IQ originals from his site instead.

### 3.3 ISF (Vidvox) in practice
- The repo is MIT and isf.video says commercial use is free. Check each file's `CREDIT`: Vidvox/zoidberg/v002 authored files are fine; `Frosted Glass` credits geeks3d, `God Rays` and `Neon` say "adapted from ofxPostProcessing GodRaysPass.cpp" (upstream licence not checked - **unverified**), `Dither-Bayer` Hugh Kennedy, `VHS Glitch` Staffan Widegarn Ahlvik, `Truchet Tile` pjkarlik, `Radial Spectrogram` icalvin102 - their licences are not in the files.
- ISF dialect seen in the files: JSON header comment, `isf_FragNormCoord`, `RENDERSIZE`, `TIME`, `IMG_NORM_PIXEL(inputImage, loc)`, `gl_FragColor`, `"PERSISTENT"` buffers + `"PASSES"` for feedback. Doc page confirms `TIME`, `TIMEDELTA`, `RENDERSIZE`, `PASSINDEX`, `FRAMEINDEX`, `isf_FragNormCoord` ([docs.isf.video variables](https://docs.isf.video/ref_variables.html)); `IMG_*` meanings come from reading the shader files, not that page.
- Unsafe for stage content: `Strobe.fs`, `RGB Strobe.fs`, flicker-type glitches (flash limits in `concert-visuals-research/README.md` section 4).

### 3.4 GLSL Sandbox
- The only licence discussion is [issue #14](https://github.com/mrdoob/glsl-sandbox/issues/14) (open since 2012) and [issue #52](https://github.com/mrdoob/glsl-sandbox/issues/52): contributors proposed "public domain unless the author declares a licence" but it was never implemented; the 2019 answer to "can I use these commercially?" was "my interpretation is that it's not legal to use unlicensed materials ... contact the authors". A forum claim that the site is "public domain by default" is not supported by the issue.

### 3.5 The Book of Shaders / hg_sdf
- Book of Shaders: see table; the repo's GitHub licence field is "NOASSERTION" and README still says "All rights reserved". Treat every example as read-only learning material. Rewrite from the underlying maths if needed.
- hg_sdf is CC BY-NC (no commercial). The functions are standard SDF folds; IQ's articles cover most of the same ground under MIT (`opRepetition`, `opSymX`, etc. in [distfunctions](https://iquilezles.org/articles/distfunctions/), [sdfrepetition](https://iquilezles.org/articles/sdfrepetition/)). Mercury invites licence requests in the header ("Your license sucks for me. What should we change it to?"), so a written "commercial OK" email is realistic.

### 3.6 Bonzomatic / VEDA / Revision
- Bonzomatic itself is public-domain-style. The shaders people write in it (Shader Showdown, demoscene prods) are theirs. I did not verify any Revision or Shader Showdown archive with a licence, so **none is on the pick list**. VEDA is an MIT Atom plugin (idle since 2022-09) and not a shader library.

## 5. Ranked recommendations for PF26

1. **Chrome/liquid background family:** #2 psrdnoise (loopable, gradient = fake normal) -> #3 warp -> #4 palette/#16 Oklab for plum-to-gold, optional #12 iridescence on top. Cheap (single pass; cost class L-M, estimate), matches Safe/Hold/Artist banks.
2. **Prism / glass hero:** #8 `triPrismSDF` + #9 smooth-min + #10 normals/shadows on the #11 scaffold, with #13 three-IOR dispersion and #15 spectral for the rainbow fan. This is the expensive path (H); keep as a hero ident only, and measure at 1080x1920 first (not yet measured).
3. **Post chain for all looks:** #20 chromatic aberration, #22 glow (or TD Bloom), #25 grain; each is a single pass.
4. **Kaleido / tunnel / feedback for Artist and Moments:** #18, #19, #21 (reference logic), driven from TD's Feedback TOP.
5. **Transitions:** #24 gl-transitions (cube, crosswarp, kaleidoscope) - ready-made, MIT per file.
6. **Do not use without permission:** Shadertoy default-licence shaders, Book of Shaders, hg_sdf, GLSL Sandbox, lygia files with Prosperity or no header. If a specific Shadertoy look is wanted, the route is: user decides -> I/agent drafts a polite request -> author's written yes saved in the ledger -> then port.
7. **Ledger:** `shader_licences.md` (or a header block in each GLSL DAT) with columns: our name, source URL, author, licence text snippet, date read, who verified. Keep the MIT/BSD notices in the shader text.

## 6. Porting cheat-sheet (TD 2025.31550, macOS/Rosetta)

Already verified in this repo: no `#version` line; `out vec4 fragColor; fragColor = TDOutputSwizzle(...)`; `vUV.st`; `uTDOutputInfo.res` (z/w = width/height); time through a vec uniform (`vec0name`, `vec0valuex` expression `absTime.seconds`); GLSL Version par offers 430-460 and `__VERSION__` evaluated to 430 default / 460 selected ([A section 6, item 2](A_td_2025_howto.md)); a GLSL TOP has only 3 input connectors, extra TOPs via the `tops` par numbered after the wired ones, `uniform` arrays unrolled ([td-port/LEARNINGS.md](../../../td-port/LEARNINGS.md)).
From TD docs ([Write a GLSL TOP](https://docs.derivative.ca/Write_a_GLSL_TOP), [GLSL TOP](https://docs.derivative.ca/GLSL_TOP)): `sTD2DInputs[]` (index = n-th 2D input), `uTD2DInfos[i].res` = (1/w, 1/h, w, h), `TD_NUM_2D_INPUTS`, `TDOutputSwizzle()` before writing, `TDDither()`, `uTDPass` with Num Passes, `#extension` lines go in the Preprocess Directives DAT, `#include` in the main shader only.

| Source construct | TD equivalent |
|---|---|
| Shadertoy `void mainImage(out vec4 O, in vec2 U)` | write `main()` that calls it: `vec4 c; mainImage(c, vUV.st*uTDOutputInfo.res.zw); fragColor = TDOutputSwizzle(c);` (this mapping is my inference, not from a Derivative page - no Derivative Shadertoy guide found; the only Shadertoy-to-TD item seen is a [Jack DiLaura tutorial post](https://derivative.ca/community-post/tutorial/glitchy-trails-glsl-touchdesigner/68789) without code mapping) |
| `iResolution` | `vec3(uTDOutputInfo.res.zw, 1.0)` |
| `iTime`, `iFrame`, `iMouse` | custom uniforms (vec0name... or custom pars); `absTime.seconds`, `absTime.frame` |
| `iChannel0..3` | `sTD2DInputs[0..2]` (+ `tops` par for more); mipmaps are not automatic |
| Common / Buffer A-D tabs | paste Common at top; each Buffer = its own GLSL TOP, with Feedback TOP for self-reference |
| ISF `isf_FragNormCoord`, `RENDERSIZE`, `TIME`, `IMG_NORM_PIXEL(img, uv)`, `gl_FragColor`, `PERSISTENT` buffers | `vUV.st`, `uTDOutputInfo.res.zw`, uniform, `texture(sTD2DInputs[i], uv)`, `fragColor`, Feedback TOP |
| `texture2D`, `gl_FragColor`, `varying`, `attribute` | `texture`, `fragColor`, not valid in the TD pixel shader. The offline checker in td-port used GL 4.1 core as a conservative bound (see A, B6) |
| variable named **`active`** | rename (reserved word; hit in td-port, renamed `enabledFx`, [LEARNINGS](../../../td-port/LEARNINGS.md)). The GLSL spec also reserves words such as `input`, `output`, `filter`, `sample`, `half`, `fixed`, `common`, `partition` - **from the spec, not probed here**; rename any such identifier before debugging anything else |
| large time seeds / sine hashes | td-port saw a sine-based GLSL hash give unchanged output; use #7 Hoskins hash and wrap time |
| precision | default 8-bit output; feedback and gradients want 16/32-bit float formats; Syphon out is 8-bit RGBA only ([A](A_td_2025_howto.md)) |
| portrait 1080x1920 | Shadertoy-style `(2.*U - res)/res.y` works; zoom/scale to taste. Seamless loop: phase = 2*pi*t/T through periodic noise (psrdnoise periods) |
| flashing | no strobes/hard alternation; follow `concert-visuals-research/README.md` section 4 |
